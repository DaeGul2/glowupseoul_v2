Getting started

What does a beauty concierge actually do?

Think of me as a bridge between you and clinics in Seoul. If you’re unsure about pricing, or there’s something you’d like to work on, I can help you find doctors and clinics that suit you, and stay with you through it.

Is it really free? How do you make money?

It’s free for you. Partner clinics pay us a referral fee, and only after your treatment is done. You’d pay the clinic directly, at the same price as any walk-in patient.

Do I need to know what treatment I want?

Not at all. It can help to have a rough idea, since most clinics have someone whose job is to sell and their advice can pull you in different directions. If you’re not sure yet, just tell me your concern and we can figure it out together.

How far in advance should I message you?

If you can, messaging 2–4 weeks before your trip usually gives us enough time for skin treatments, and a little earlier for surgery. Already in Seoul? Feel free to message anyway and I’ll see what’s possible.

Clinics & safety

How do you know a clinic is trustworthy?

Certifications, how many cases a clinic has done and government recognition are good places to start. Personally, I look for clinics where the doctors listen, explain things and genuinely want to solve your problem rather than treat you as a one-time customer. Those are the clinics I’d suggest to you.

Treatments

It’s my first treatment in Korea. What would you suggest?

It really depends on your skin and what you’d like to change, so I’d love to hear your concern first. If you’d like a gentle start, many first-timers enjoy a skin booster. It suits most skin types and people often notice a glow within two to three weeks.

Isn’t botox just for wrinkles?

Not always. Outside Korea most people know botox for wrinkles, but here you’ll also hear about “skin botox”, which is used more like a skin booster for pores and a smoother, glassy look. It’s worth asking your doctor which kind they mean.

Botox or skin boosters?

They do different jobs, so some people end up choosing both. Botox is often used to soften or prevent wrinkles, while a skin booster gives your skin more nourishment. Your doctor can help you decide based on your concern.

Skin booster or laser?

As a rough guide, skin boosters work on your skin overall, so they can be a nice choice if you’re not sure what you need. Lasers usually target something specific, so I’d suggest deciding on those after talking with a doctor.

Price

Why not book the cheapest clinic?

It’s worth looking beyond the lowest price. Sometimes the cheapest option on a menu is there to bring you in, and once other treatments are added the total can end up similar or higher. Comparing the total for everything you’d actually get usually gives a fairer picture.

They’re reference prices from partner clinics to help you plan. You’d get a full written quote before anything is booked.

Usually you’d pay the clinic directly, by card or cash, on the day. You won’t pay us anything.

Your trip

Gangnam or Apgujeong?

As a general rule, Gangnam has more skin clinics and Apgujeong has more plastic surgeons. For skin treatments Gangnam is a good place to start, and for surgery you might look at Apgujeong. I’m happy to suggest an area once I know your goal.

▶ Watch Romie answer this
Can I come alone?

Yes, many patients come on their own. Your coordinator can be with you at the consultation and treatment, and there when you wake up from surgery.

How many days do I need in Seoul?

For most skin treatments, 1–2 days in Seoul is often enough. For surgery, around 7–10 days is a safer plan, and most surgeons advise not flying for 48–72 hours afterwards. Your doctor will confirm what applies to you.

I don’t speak Korean. Is that a problem?

Not at all. Your coordinator can interpret for you before, during and after your treatment. We speak English, 中文 and Bahasa.

Do I need a medical visa?

For short skin treatments, a regular tourist entry is often enough. For a longer surgical stay, it’s worth checking with us first and we’ll look at what applies to your passport.

Aftercare

Is it normal to still have bumps after treatment?

It can depend on your skin type, and it sometimes takes 1–2 weeks to settle. If you’re worried, send me a photo and I can check with the clinic.

Do you still help after I fly home?

Yes. We like to check in on day 1, 7 and 30, and you’re welcome to message any time, even after you’ve left Korea.
